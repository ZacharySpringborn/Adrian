Why it beats regular iPhone dictation

Apple's dictation types exactly what you say — every "um," every restart, no structure. Wispr Flow listens to what you meant: it drops filler words, fixes your mid-sentence corrections, adds punctuation, and turns spoken lists into real lists. It also adjusts tone, so a text to a friend reads casual and an email reads professional.

1 Finish the iPhone setup

Make the Flow keyboard your voice button everywhere

If you only installed the app, you're halfway there. The magic happens when Flow is a keyboard, so it shows up in Messages, Mail, Gmail, Slack, Claude — anywhere you can type.

Add the keyboard

  1. Open Settings → General → Keyboard → Keyboards.
  2. Tap Add New Keyboard and pick Wispr Flow.
  3. Tap Wispr Flow in the list, turn on Allow Full Access, then Allow.
  4. In any app, tap the 🌐 globe key (or long-press it) to switch to Flow.

Set your default style

  1. Open the Wispr Flow app → Style tab.
  2. Pick your default: Formal, Casual, Very casual or Excited.
  3. For business, I'd start with Formal or Casual. You can always change it.

Heads-up: Flow needs Full Access to work as a keyboard. That's normal for third-party keyboards — just switch back to the regular keyboard when you're typing passwords or account numbers.

3 Learn the four tricks

Talk like a person; let Flow format

① Lists happen by themselves

Three things for tomorrow: one, call the insurance guy; two, send Jake the invoice; three, order the parts. Three things for tomorrow:
1. Call the insurance guy
2. Send Jake the invoice
3. Order the parts

② Change your mind mid-sentence

Let's meet Thursday at 10, actually make it 11. Let's meet Thursday at 11.

"Actually," "scratch that," and "never mind" all work.

③ Teach it your names

In the Flow app, open Dictionary and add business names, employees, street names, product names — anything it keeps misspelling. You can also add a correction ("Adrien" → "Adrian").

④ Snippets = shortcuts you speak

In Snippets, save a trigger phrase and the text it expands to. Say "my booking link" and your full calendar link appears. Great for your address, signature, standard replies, or your pricing blurb.

Also handy: say "new paragraph," "new line," or "question mark" when you want them exactly.

4 The big one: talk to Claude

Brain-dump out loud, get back a clean action list

This is how I use it most. Open the Claude app, tap in the message box, switch to the Flow keyboard, and just ramble — everything on your mind, in any order. Flow makes it readable; Claude pulls out the salient points and organizes them. You don't have to think in bullet points. That's Claude's job.

What I say (roughly 60 seconds of talking)

"Okay, here's everything rattling around. The Henderson job needs a revised quote by Friday — the materials went up. I promised Sarah I'd look at the new hire's schedule. Need to renew the van insurance, I think it's this month. Call the accountant about quarterlies. And I want to post something about the spring promo but I haven't figured out the angle. Turn this into a to-do list grouped by business, put deadlines first, and tell me what I can delegate."

The bold line at the end is the instruction. Everything before it can be as messy as you want.

Instructions worth stealing (just say them at the end)

  • "…turn this into action items with owners and due dates."
  • "…what are the three things I should do first today and why?"
  • "…ask me any questions you need before you organize this." (Claude will interview you — surprisingly useful.)
  • "…I just got out of a meeting with ___. Here's what we discussed. Pull out decisions, follow-ups, and anything I promised."

Tip: start a Claude Project for each business so Claude remembers the context (clients, pricing, how you like things written) and you're not re-explaining every time.

5 Email

Two ways to knock out email by voice

A. Quick replies: dictate straight in

Open Mail or Gmail, hit reply, switch to Flow, talk. Flow cleans it up and uses your email tone. Done in 20 seconds. Use your signature snippet to finish.

Hi Tom, thanks for sending this over. Tuesday works, let's say 2 o'clock at the site. I'll bring the revised numbers. Talk soon.

B. Tricky emails: talk it through with Claude

For anything sensitive — pushing back on a price, a late payment, a delicate client — tell Claude the situation out loud and let it draft.

"Draft a reply to this customer. He's upset the job ran two days long. It was the supplier's fault, but I want to own it, offer 10% off the labor, and keep him for the next project. Firm but warm, short." (then paste his email)

Read it, tweak it, copy it into your email. You just saved 15 minutes of staring at a blank screen.

6 Documents

Talk the rough draft; let Claude build the document

Proposals, job descriptions, how-we-do-it checklists for employees, a one-page plan — these are where voice + Claude saves the most time. You know the content; you just hate formatting it. So don't.

  1. Say what it is and who it's for. "I need a one-page proposal for a commercial cleaning contract, for the office manager at a dental practice."
  2. Dump everything you know — scope, pricing, schedule, what makes you different — in whatever order it comes out.
  3. Ask for structure. "Organize this into a proposal with sections, keep it under a page, and list anything I forgot to mention."
  4. Edit by voice. "Make the pricing section a table." "Shorter intro." "Sound less salesy."

That last line — "list anything I forgot" — is gold. Claude will catch the missing insurance clause or the payment terms you didn't say out loud.

Two desktop extras worth knowing

  • Command Mode (turn on under Settings → Experimental): highlight a paragraph, hold the shortcut, and say "make this more professional" or "turn this into bullet points." It rewrites in place.
  • Notetaker + Claude: Flow can take notes on your computer meetings, and you can connect it to Claude (Flow Settings → MCP). Then you can ask Claude, "What did I promise in yesterday's call with the supplier?" without pasting a transcript.

What a day of this looks like

You don't need to do all of it. Even just the morning dump changes things.

Morning
Two-minute brain dump to Claude before you start: "Here's everything on my plate today… what are the top three?"
Between stops
Action Button → talk → done. Reply to texts and quick emails by voice instead of thumbs.
After meetings
Voice recap to Claude: "Just met with ___. Here's what happened. Pull out the follow-ups." Paste them into your to-do app or calendar.
At the desk
Draft the hard stuff by talking: proposals, the email you've been avoiding, the employee checklist.
End of day
Wrap-up: "Here's what got done and what didn't. Build tomorrow's list."

Free or Pro?

Free — $0

  • Works in every app, on phone and computer
  • Dictionary and snippets included
  • Weekly word cap — roughly 1,000–2,000 words a week depending on the device, about 10 minutes of talking

Plenty for trying it out. If you're doing brain dumps and emails daily, you'll hit the cap by midweek.

Pro — $15/mo, or $12/mo billed yearly

  • Unlimited dictation — this is the whole point
  • Command Mode on the computer
  • Longer meeting-note history, priority support, early features

My take: I pay for it, and it's one of the easier subscriptions to justify. If it saves you even 20 minutes a month, it's paid for itself — and it'll save you way more than that.
